About Iraq

Iraq (Arabic: العراق, al-ʿIrāq), officially the Republic of Iraq, is a country in Western Asia. It borders Turkey to the north, Iran to the east, Kuwait and the Persian Gulf to the southeast, Saudi Arabia to the south, Jordan to the southwest and Syria to the west. Its capital and largest city is Baghdad.

Iraq is home to ancient Mesopotamia, the land between the Tigris and Euphrates rivers. Many historians call it the "Cradle of Civilization", because writing, the wheel, the first cities and the first written laws appeared here thousands of years ago.

Today Iraq is a federal parliamentary republic with a young and growing population. It has one of the largest oil reserves in the world, and a rich mix of peoples, languages and religions.

History

Ancient Mesopotamia

Around 3500 BC the Sumerians built some of the world's first cities, such as Ur and Uruk. They invented cuneiform, one of the earliest writing systems. Later came the Akkadians, the Babylonians and the Assyrians.

King Hammurabi of Babylon (about 1750 BC) wrote one of the first sets of laws in history, the Code of Hammurabi. Later, King Nebuchadnezzar II made Babylon one of the greatest cities of the ancient world, famous for the Ishtar Gate and the legendary Hanging Gardens.

The Islamic Golden Age

In 762 AD the Abbasid Caliph al-Mansur founded Baghdad, the "Round City". Baghdad became the center of the Islamic Golden Age. The House of Wisdom (Bayt al-Hikma) gathered scientists, mathematicians and translators. The word algorithm comes from the name of the Baghdad scholar al-Khwarizmi — very important for IT!

Modern Iraq

  1. 1921 – The Kingdom of Iraq is established.
  2. 1932 – Iraq becomes independent and joins the League of Nations.
  3. 1958 – The monarchy ends and Iraq becomes a republic.
  4. 2005 – A new constitution is approved by referendum.
  5. Today – Iraq continues to rebuild and develop.

Geography & Nature

Iraq has many different landscapes: green mountains in the north (Kurdistan Region), wide fertile plains in the center, deserts in the west, and the famous Mesopotamian Marshes in the south, where the Tigris and Euphrates join to form the Shatt al-Arab.

  • Climate: very hot summers (often above 45°C) and mild winters.
  • Highest point: Cheekha Dar, about 3,611 m, on the border with Iran.
  • Famous tree: the date palm – Iraq was once the world's largest date producer.
  • UNESCO site: the Ahwar (Marshes) of Southern Iraq, added in 2016.

Major Cities

Baghdad

The capital and largest city, on the Tigris River. Famous for Al-Mutanabbi Street, the street of books.

Basra

Iraq's main port city in the south, on the Shatt al-Arab. Known as the "Venice of the East".

Erbil

Capital of the Kurdistan Region. Its Citadel is one of the oldest continuously inhabited places on Earth.

Mosul

A historic city in the north, near the ruins of ancient Nineveh, capital of Assyria.

Famous Landmarks

Malwiya Minaret, Samarra

A 52-meter spiral minaret built in the 9th century.

Arch of Ctesiphon (Taq Kasra)

One of the largest brick arches ever built, from the Sasanian era.

Imam Husayn Shrine, Karbala

One of the most visited holy places in the world.

Al-Mustansiriya, Baghdad

A university founded in 1227, one of the oldest in the world.

Culture & Food

Iraqi culture is a mix of Arab, Kurdish, Turkmen, Assyrian and many other traditions. Iraqis are well known for their hospitality – guests are always welcomed with tea and food.

Music & Poetry

Iraq has a long tradition of poetry and the Iraqi Maqam, a classical music style. Famous Iraqi poets include al-Mutanabbi, Badr Shakir al-Sayyab and Nazik al-Malaika.

Famous Iraqi Food

Masgouf

The national dish: carp fish grilled slowly next to an open fire.

Dolma

Vegetables and vine leaves stuffed with rice and meat.

Kleicha

The national cookie, filled with dates and spices.
